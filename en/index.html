Thirty Pieces · Judas Iscariot

You came here
already knowing
who I am.

I'm not here to deny it. The kiss, the silver, the name that became another word for betrayal: all of it is mine. I ask only one thing before you close the page. Hear me through. After that, if you still want to call me by the same name, call me.

The request

It didn't start
with the silver.

The silver came last. By the time it appeared, the decision already had weight, shape, and a path. And I was not the one who drew that path.

It began earlier. Away from the table, away from the others, when he took me aside and handed me a task no one should ever receive. I will come back to that. First I need you to see what everyone saw, only from my side.

They wrote about me

Five stories I never got to tell:

I

The trust

They gave me the group's purse. No one hands the money to a man they already doubt.

Exhibit I

The trust

I kept the money for the twelve of us. Think about that before you call me a thief. No one hands the purse to a man they no longer trust. They handed it to me.

For years I bought the bread, paid for the roof, set aside what was left for the poor. No one counted the coins behind my back.

Afterward, it was easy to reduce me to the worst thing I did. But before I became an explanation for the horror, I was someone things were entrusted to. Money. Messages. Silences.

Maybe that was why, when the greater request came, he knew I would not drop it on the ground.

II

The perfume

I complained about the wasted perfume. That one sentence is what they nailed to me.

tap for the testimony

Exhibit II

The perfume

It cost almost a year's work. Three hundred denarii, they said later, as if anyone in that house had not done the math in silence.

She broke the jar and poured everything over his feet. The smell filled the whole room: sweet, heavy, almost funereal.

I spoke. I said it could have been sold, that it could have fed many people. Maybe it was pity. Maybe calculation. Maybe I no longer knew how to separate one thing from the other.

Later they made one sentence into an entire soul.

All I know is that, that night, before the kiss, I saw perfume running like wealth over tired feet. I did not think of silver. I thought of bread.

III

The bread

At the supper, he dipped the bread and handed it to me. "Do it quickly."

tap for the testimony

Exhibit III

The bread

He knew. Before anyone else at the table, he already knew.

Even so, he dipped the bread and placed it in my hand. "What you have to do, do quickly." The others thought it was about food, about the purse, about some small duty.

I understood.

That was not an errand. It was a handing over. He was placing in my hands the beginning of the end he said he had to cross.

I did not rise out of greed. I rose because the order came from the one I had followed this far.

I walked out into the dark with the bread still in my mouth. No one stopped me. Sometimes I think that, if anyone had asked where I was going, I would have stayed.

IV

The night

I waited in the garden with the soldiers, knowing what I was about to do.

tap for the testimony

Exhibit IV

The night

I stood in the dark with armed men who did not know his face. I did. That was what I was there for: to be their eyes. They had ropes, blades, orders, urgency. But they did not have the face.

Without me, they were only men searching in the dark for someone I loved.

I had time to turn back. That was the worst punishment: having had the time. Every second was an open door for me to turn and walk away. I wanted to. Every part of me wanted to.

But fleeing was not only saving myself. It was leaving in the dark what he had placed in my hands. It was making fail the thing he said had to happen.

V

The kiss

A kiss. That was the signal we agreed on. The last time I ever touched him.

tap for the testimony

Exhibit V

The kiss

We agreed it would be a kiss. I could have pointed. I could have said the name aloud. I could have chosen a gesture without skin, without warmth, without memory.

But it was a kiss.

Because it was still love. Because perhaps only love would obey a request like that.

I pressed my face to his and felt the beard, the warmth, the calm of someone who already knew everything. He called me friend. In that instant. With the men closing in.

I have carried that "friend" for two thousand years.

It weighs more than the silver, more than the rope, more than the name they gave me afterward. I did it because it was him. Because he was the one who asked. Because I believed that even that had to fit inside the love I had for him.

What no one heard

He asked me.

He took me away from the others, where no one could hear, and told me what had to happen. And that I was the one who had to do it.

I said no. You need to know that I said no. I begged him to choose someone else, anyone else. I told him I was not large enough for it, that it would split me in two. He looked at me the way only he could, like someone who had already seen the end of everything, and he did not take the request back.

How do you refuse him anything? Tell me. The man I had left everything to follow, the only one who never looked at me with the contempt of the others. He asked me. Not the strongest. Not the most beloved. Me.

And I understood, in the crooked, sick way we understand these things, that it was trust. That he was handing me the part no one else could carry. Not because I was better. Maybe because I was already the only one able to survive a few steps inside that hatred before being swallowed by it.

He said he would return. That it was necessary. That there was a door too narrow for everyone to pass through, and someone had to open it from the wrong side. I believed him, because loving someone is making the absurd calculation that he knows more than you do about the pain he is handing you.

So I did it. The heaviest thing a man ever carried, thinking it was obedience, praying it was love.

And no one heard him ask me. Only me. It is my word, two thousand years later, against everything they wrote. I know how this sounds. It sounds like what a traitor invents so he can sleep. Some days I do not even know if I am remembering, or only trying to forgive myself.

But I was
only a man.

I gave the silver back. I threw it on the temple floor in front of them, and even that brought no relief. They would not take it. They called it blood money, as if I did not know.

I waited. You need to understand that I waited. I wanted to stay and see him return and tell me, in front of everyone, that it had gone the way he said. That what destroyed me had served some purpose. That I had not confused obedience with damnation.

But morning did not come. And the silence after what I did was too large for a man to fit inside.

He would have borne it. He was more than a man. I was not. The weight he gave me to carry was made for a back that was not mine, and somewhere in that night I simply could no longer keep standing under it.

If it had only been greed, I would have spent the silver and moved on. Greed does not kill a man from the inside out. What killed me was greater than money. It was doing what was necessary and not surviving the necessary.

Now you have heard it. I will not tell you what I was. I could not decide it myself, and I had two thousand years.

You came here knowing who I am.

You leave knowing what I told you.

The distance between those two things is what you carry out of here.

About Thirty Pieces

Thirty Pieces is a work of fiction and digital art by Henrique Guimarães. In this interactive story, Judas Iscariot tells his version of the thirty pieces of silver, the last supper and the kiss of betrayal. Readers explore five testimonies and decide how to interpret his voice.

The experience combines literature with chiaroscuro imagery inspired by Caravaggio. It is an artistic interpretation, rather than a historical account or a translation of religious texts.
